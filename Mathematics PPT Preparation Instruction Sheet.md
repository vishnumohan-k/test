# Mathematics PPT Preparation Instruction Sheet

## Purpose

Use this guide to turn a supplied chapter PDF into clear, accurate, classroom-ready mathematics PowerPoint presentations. The chapter PDF is the source of truth; the reference presentations in this folder illustrate the design and teaching approach to follow or avoid.

## Target students and age-appropriate teaching

The presentations are for students from **Class 1 through Class 10**. Keep explanations simple, engaging, and suitable for the specific class—not generic across all ages.

- Use the grade or standard stated in the PDF to choose vocabulary, examples, notation, reading load, and mathematical depth.
- For younger students, favour concrete objects, familiar situations, clear pictures, short instructions, and one small step at a time.
- For older students, introduce appropriate symbols, abstraction, multi-step reasoning, and relevant applications while keeping explanations clear and uncluttered.
- Keep examples and visual style respectful of students’ maturity: engaging does not mean childish, and simple does not mean mathematically shallow.
- If the grade cannot be identified from the PDF, ask the user before choosing a consequential level of difficulty.

## Input and expected output

**Input:** A PDF containing one or more mathematics chapters.

**Default output:** One editable `.pptx` presentation per assigned chapter. Follow the eight-slide structure below. Add a slide only when it is genuinely needed to keep the content clear and readable. Provide a clear file name for each chapter. Include a PDF preview only if requested or useful for review.

## Recommended teaching structure

Organize each chapter around the three learning pillars:

1. **Understand** — Explain the concept or rule simply and age-appropriately. Use a worked example and explain why or how it works, not only the final answer.
2. **Visualise** — Clearly represent a method, relationship, pattern, or process. A worked-example flow, number or pattern sequence, array, concept map, decision tree, or solving path can work; it need not be a traditional flowchart.
3. **Recall and revise** — Prompt active recall with questions or cues, then provide matching answers or definitions. Finish with key takeaways and a check for understanding.

Use these pillars in the slide sequence below. Do not add a section merely to fill the structure.

## Build the content from the PDF

- Read the whole supplied chapter and identify its title, grade or level, learning goals, key terms, methods, examples, exercises, and page numbers.
- Keep the presentation within the supplied chapter and requested scope. Do not invent curriculum requirements, facts, questions, or answer keys.
- Preserve the source’s mathematical meaning, notation, units, and level of difficulty. Simplify wording, not the mathematics.
- If the grade, chapter boundaries, or a necessary part of the source is unclear, ask the user or clearly flag the limitation rather than guessing.
- Select representative material; do not copy every paragraph or exercise onto slides. Write explanations and questions in your own words rather than copying textbook wording or questions word-for-word.
- Use only images included in the supplied materials or textbook images. Do not use images from the internet or other external sources. Original diagrams made for the presentation are encouraged.
- Keep questions and solutions together as a deliberate sequence: show the prompt first, give students time to think, then reveal or present the worked answer on a later slide.

## Mathematics-specific rules

- On the concept slide, include a clear “What is …?” question when appropriate and highlight one key idea, formula, rule, or relationship.
- On the real-world analogy or “Think About It” slide, connect the concept to a familiar example or prompt and end with concise key takeaways.
- On the mini-case slide, use a relatable observation, situation, or activity. Where suitable, guide students through **Observe → Compare → Explain** and state the learning point.
- Show worked solutions one meaningful step at a time. State the operation or rule being used when it is not obvious; do not jump from question to answer.
- Check every calculation, sign, symbol, exponent, fraction, decimal, unit, and final answer. Independently recalculate examples and answer keys.
- Typeset equations cleanly and at a readable size. Keep notation consistent from slide to slide; avoid screenshots of blurry equations or long inline formula strings.
- Use representations that support the concept: counters or arrays for counting and multiplication; number lines for order and operations; fraction bars or area models for fractions; labelled diagrams for geometry; tables and correctly scaled axes for data.
- Label diagrams, graphs, axes, units, and important values. Make clear when a drawing is not to scale. Do not let decorative illustrations obscure the mathematical relationship.
- Include at least one fully explained example for a new procedure, followed by a question students can attempt independently or with a partner.
- Where relevant, surface a likely misconception with a short “notice” or “check” prompt and explain how to avoid it.
- Use examples appropriate to the learners’ age and context. A real-world connection should clarify the mathematics, not distract from it.

## Required eight-slide structure

Follow this sequence for each chapter. Keep the deck to eight slides unless an additional slide is genuinely needed to keep the content clear and readable.

| Slide | Section | What to include |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening / Overview** | Grade or standard, subject, topic or chapter, and an introduction to **01 Understand, 02 Visualise, 03 Recall & Revise**. |
| 2 | **Pillar 1 — Simplified Concept** | A simple, age-appropriate explanation; a clear “What is …?” question where appropriate; one key idea, formula, rule, or relationship. |
| 3 | **Pillar 1 — Real-World Analogy / Think About It** | A familiar experience, example, or prompt connected to the concept; concise key takeaways for quick revision. |
| 4 | **Pillar 1 — Mini Case Study** | A relatable observation, situation, or activity. Use **Observe → Compare → Explain** where suitable and make the learning point clear. |
| 5 | **Pillar 2 — Visual Representation** | A clear visual of a process, sequence, relationship, pattern, rule, or method. Use numbered steps, connected boxes, an array, a worked-example flow, or another suitable structure; add a one-line explanation for each step or box. |
| 6 | **Pillar 3 — Flashcards: Front** | Questions, prompts, key terms, or recall cues only. Do not show the answers on this slide. |
| 7 | **Pillar 3 — Flashcards: Back** | The matching answers or definitions in the same order and with the same numbering as Slide 6. Do not add, omit, or reorder cards. |
| 8 | **Review — Key Takeaways & Quick Check** | Concise takeaways and five check questions. Include sequencing, application, or understanding questions where appropriate; verify every answer. |

## Slide design and classroom usability

- Use PowerPoint `.pptx` format and a 16:9 widescreen layout on every slide. Use Aptos or Arial, consistent formatting, clear alignment, spacing, and strong contrast.
- Make the title state the slide’s purpose. Use the guideline’s size ranges: slide titles 28–36 pt, headings 22–28 pt, body text 14–24 pt, and footnotes or labels at least 12 pt. Keep equations and diagrams readable when projected.
- Keep each slide focused on one main idea. Prefer a few short bullets, a worked example, or a purposeful visual over dense paragraphs.
- Give diagrams and mathematical working enough space. Avoid crowding, tiny labels, excessive decoration, and unnecessary transitions or animation.
- Use colour consistently to distinguish quantities or steps, but never rely on colour alone to communicate meaning.
- Include brief opportunities to participate: predict, solve, compare strategies, explain a step, or check an answer. Keep teacher prompts concise and actionable.
- Use a cover that identifies the subject, grade or level, chapter, and a concise learning hook. Avoid a contents slide unless it helps students navigate a genuinely long presentation.

## Avoid (patterns from the not-recommended references)

- Text-heavy slides that reproduce long explanations instead of teaching one idea at a time.
- Repeating the same heading, summary, or instructions across multiple slides without adding new learning.
- Crowded agendas or lists that name many sections but do not help students understand or practise the mathematics.
- Combining unrelated topics or activities in a way that hides the chapter’s learning progression.
- Decorative images, clip art, or effects that compete with the explanation or make a diagram harder to read.
- Unexplained answer-only examples, questions without a way to check learning, or answer slides that do not match their prompts.
- Unsupported claims, made-up examples presented as source content, and unverified or inconsistent calculations.

## Final quality check

Before delivery, verify that:

- Every chapter in scope has a deck, and each deck covers the right chapter and learner level.
- The explanations, examples, diagrams, calculations, units, and answer keys are mathematically correct and consistent with the PDF.
- Each new idea is explained before students are asked to use it; questions have a clear answer or checking method.
- Text, equations, graphs, and labels are readable in presentation mode and are not clipped or overlapping.
- The deck follows the required sequence and includes all required content, with no filler or needless repetition.
- The content is appropriate for the learners’ age and grade, accurate and consistent across slides, and written in the author’s own words.
- There is no confidential or internal information; the deck follows applicable AI and information-safety rules.
- The `.pptx` files open successfully and remain editable. If a PDF preview is requested, check that it matches the slides.
- Formatting is consistent and the file is named using the required pattern: `BoardName-Subject-Standard-ChapterNumber-DeloitteUsername.pptx` (for example, `Bihar-Mathematics-Std-1-Ch-3-[username].pptx`).
- If submission is requested and access is available, upload the completed presentation to the designated MS Teams location, following Cluster or Location Lead instructions.

## Request pattern

When the user shares a chapter PDF, use this instruction sheet to create one presentation per assigned chapter. If important details are missing and cannot be established from the PDF, ask before making consequential assumptions.

**User input:** “Share the PDF document with chapters for PPT.”

**Expected output:** Editable PowerPoint presentation(s) for the chapters in that PDF, prepared according to this instruction sheet.
